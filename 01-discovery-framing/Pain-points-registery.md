## From Evidence to Pain Point (Example)

| Stage | Content |
|---|---|
| Raw Message | "Trainee did not receive the project-group email." |
| Observed Issue | The trainee did not receive the group invitation. |
| Pain Point | Trainees may fail to access their assigned project group after team allocation. |
| Possible Root Cause (Hypothesis) | Manual group allocation / incorrect contact data / no confirmation mechanism. |

> ⚠️ The root cause above is still a hypothesis and needs to be validated through further analysis (5 Whys / Fishbone).

## Why Prioritize?
Not all 15 pain points carry equal weight. Rather than analyzing all of them with the same depth, prioritization ensures:
- Focus stays on high-value problems
- Objective criteria replace personal opinion
- Time and resources are used efficiently
- Some problems may be symptoms of larger, underlying issues

> "Prioritization doesn't mean ignoring the other problems. It means we start with the highest-impact problems."
